Seven years ago, during my years as a Master student at TUM, there was a fellow, who actually was a physicist, who told me about the Reynolds transport theorem, which essentially is differentiation under the integral in case of varying integration domain. At that time, I recall that I wanted to write up a nice simple proof of that theorem in differential geometric language. Now, 7 years later, finally the time came, and I want to share this proof here.

## the assertion

Let $\omega_t$ be a time-dependent $n$-form. Let $U_t$ be a time-dependent $n$-dimensional submanifold with boundary, in the sense that there is a time-dependent vector field $X_t$ such that $U_t = \varphi^t_0(U_0)$. Here $\varphi^t_s$ denotes the non-autonomous flow of $X_t$, such that the lower index tells us at which time we start the time-dependence, i.e. $\varphi^0_s = \id$. Then

$$
\begin{equation}

\label{reynolds}

\frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \int_{U_t} \omega_t = \int_{U_\tau} i_{X_{t + \tau}} \d \omega_\tau + \int_{\partial U_\tau} i_{X_{t+\tau}} \omega_\tau + \int_{U_\tau} \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \omega_t.

\end{equation}
$$

## the proof

It turns out that Reynolds transport theorem essentially amounts to change of variables combined with Stokes' theorem and another magic ingredient :) First,

$$
\int_{U_t} \omega_t = \int_{\varphi^t_0(U_0)} \omega_t = \int_{U_0} (\varphi^t_0)^\ast \omega_t.
$$

Then, assuming smoothness and suitable integrability, we use dominated convergence to differentiate under the integral sign

$$
\frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \int_{U_0} (\varphi^t_0)^\ast \omega_t = \int_{U_0} \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} (\varphi^t_0)^\ast \omega_t.
$$

The integrand looks almost like a Lie derivative. As usual, we have

$$
\frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} (\varphi^t_0)^\ast \omega_t = (\varphi^\tau_0)^\ast \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = 0} (\varphi^t_\tau)^\ast \omega_{t + \tau}
$$

Define $T\colon (r,s) \mapsto (\varphi^r_\tau)^\ast \omega_{s + \tau}$. A simple calculation shows that 

$$

\partial_r T(0,0) = \frac{\partial}{\partial_r}\eval_{r=0, s=0} (\varphi^r_\tau)^\ast \omega_{s + \tau} = \L_{X_{t + \tau}} \omega_\tau

$$

and

$$

\partial_s T(0,0) = (\varphi^0_\tau)^\ast \frac{\d}{\d s}\eval_{s = 0} \omega_{s + \tau} = \frac{\d}{\d s}\eval_{s = \tau} \omega_{s}.

$$

By using the chain rule, we finally get our Lie derivative plus the time derivative of the $n$-form

$$

\frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = 0} (\varphi^t_\tau)^\ast \omega_{t + \tau} = \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = 0} T(t,t) = \L_{X_{t + \tau}} \omega_\tau  +  \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \omega_{t}.

$$

We can establish

$$

\frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau}  \int_{U_t} \omega_t = \int_{U_0} (\varphi^\tau_0)^\ast \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = 0} (\varphi^t_\tau)^\ast \omega_{t + \tau} = \int_{U_0} (\varphi^\tau_0)^\ast \left( \L_{X_{t + \tau}} \omega_\tau  +  \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \omega_{t} \right) = \int_{U_\tau} \L_{X_{t + \tau}} \omega_\tau  + \int_{U_\tau} \frac{\d}{\d t}\eval_{t = \tau} \omega_{t}.

$$

Now Cartan's magic formula is calling :) 

$$

\int_{U_\tau} \L_{X_{t + \tau}} \omega_\tau  =  \int_{U_\tau} i_{X_{t + \tau}} \d \omega_\tau + \int_{U_\tau} \d i_{X_{t+\tau}} \omega_\tau

$$

and a final application of Stokes' theorem to the latter summand gives \eqref{reynolds}.
